# Benchmark Evaluation Summary

## Evaluation Protocol

To evaluate the diagnostic and generative capabilities of each model, we adopt the evaluation protocol used by \cite{li2023llava_med} and \cite{zhou2024comprehensive}, which reports classification accuracy, macro-F1, and per-condition recall. For measuring the quality of the generated responses, we use BERTScore \cite{zhang2020bertscore} and ROUGE-L \cite{lin2004rouge}, which capture semantic similarity and lexical overlap with respect to reference answers. In addition, we compute bootstrap 95% confidence intervals ($n=1000$) as recommended by \cite{tu2024generalist}, which provides a more reliable estimation of model performance given the limited evaluation set.

## Key Findings

Our skincare model, based on LLaMA 3.2 3B with multi-LoRA adapters and a DenseNet projection layer, obtains a classification accuracy of 65.7% [95% CI: 57.7--73.0] and a macro-F1 of 0.658. These results are close to those achieved by LLaVA-1.5-7B, which reaches 67.2% accuracy [59.1--75.2] and 0.694 macro-F1, even though our model uses less than half the number of parameters (3B vs 7B). The confidence intervals between the two models overlap, which means the difference in classification performance is not statistically significant. At the same time, both models clearly outperform the LLaMA 3.2 3B base (50.4% accuracy, 0.600 macro-F1), showing that the domain-specific fine-tuning and the projection layer contribute meaningfully to the results. In terms of response generation, our model shows a clear advantage. It achieves a BERTScore F1 of 0.927 [0.926--0.929] and a ROUGE-L of 0.497 [0.489--0.506], while LLaVA-1.5 reaches 0.851 [0.850--0.852] and 0.207 [0.203--0.212] respectively. This suggests that the responses generated by our model are more aligned with the reference answers, which may indicate a higher level of clinical usefulness. Finally, all models include a medical disclaimer in virtually all responses (100% for our model and LLaVA-1.5, 99.3% for the base), which is important for responsible use in a healthcare context.
